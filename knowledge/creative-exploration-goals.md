# Open-Ended Creative Exploration Goals

This document defines eight reusable continuation prompts for ambitious Sentinel exploration. Begin manually from a promising project state, then use Goals 1–6 and Goal 8 as standalone copy-and-paste workflows or Goal 7 as a hybrid loop that selects among them.

## Shared Contract

### Existing-state contract

- Begin from the Sentinel project exactly as it currently exists. Do not clear it, open an unrelated project, or rebuild its starting point merely to fit a preferred workflow.
- Inspect the live output, graph topology, node health, motion, textures, structured buffers, expressions, controls, interfaces, and useful branch points before deciding what to add or change.
- Treat the existing composition as creative material. Reuse, reinterpret, reroute, combine, simplify, or branch from its strongest elements instead of automatically replacing them.
- Preserve working state before risky experiments and keep recoverable branch points. When an experiment fails, revert, delete it, or branch from an earlier strong point rather than repairing it indefinitely.
- Respect the project's existing motion and control architecture. Do not impose a new global clock, phase system, interface, or data contract unless the evolving result demonstrates a specific need for one.
- Keep normal motion visibly running during creative review. Temporary pause, scrub, or deterministic capture is allowed when the existing system supports it, but restore ordinary live playback immediately afterward and never reshape the composition around evaluation machinery.
- Native Sentinel pipeline types, authored Modules, installed generative engines, analysis, simulation, structured data, 2D or 3D rendering, cameras, routing, feedback, compositing, post-processing, and interfaces are optional possibilities rather than a checklist.

### Exploration and evaluation discipline

- Work visibly one semantic node at a time. Give each new node only a quick compile, health, schema, and meaningful-preview check before continuing.
- A planning, analysis, data, or utility node must gain an immediate visible consumer within the same short sprint.
- A materially different candidate changes construction, silhouette, spatial organization, motion regime, representation, or another major visual capability. Palette, grading, threshold, blur, brightness, or overlays alone do not qualify.
- Batch serious capture, motion, vision, and profiling evaluation after several materially different candidates or at retained checkpoints rather than after every tweak.
- Treat vision feedback as criticism rather than permission. Exploration may be ugly or risky long enough to reveal its potential.
- Apply depth estimation only to imagery with plausible spatial evidence, optical flow only to coherent motion, and Features or tracking only to bounded detectable structure with a meaningful downstream use.
- If a direction stalls, degrades hierarchy, produces jitter without purpose, or needs corrective effects to conceal structural weakness, simplify it, revert it, delete it, or branch from an earlier strong point.
- Balance additive construction with subtraction and consolidation. Measure progress by discovered visual territory and expressive behavior rather than node count, plumbing, proof artifacts, or technical complexity.

## Goal 1 — Workshop → Composition → Performance

Build and continuously evolve the existing Sentinel composition through repeated workshop, composition, and performance loops, discovering its next concept from what the current output actually suggests rather than deciding a destination in advance. Begin by inspecting the live image, motion, graph, controls, textures, structured buffers, and branch points, then identify the smallest new tool or transformation that could produce an immediate major visible advance while preserving the strongest existing material. Use each workshop addition in a composition experiment as soon as it exists, keep only behavior that proves expressive in ordinary playback, and build interfaces only when they enable meaningful performance beyond routine Properties adjustments. Compare materially different results in batches, branch or delete weak directions, alternate addition with subtraction and consolidation, restore normal live playback after deterministic inspection, and continue until the user says to stop before polishing, profiling, proving, and saving the strongest result.

## Goal 2 — Territory Sprint → Tournament → Harvest

Rapidly push the existing Sentinel composition into several radically different visual territories before committing to its next architecture, then compare those territories and harvest only their strongest discoveries. First inspect the current output and choose a few promising branch points, preserving the working state while each branch attempts a major change in construction, silhouette, spatial organization, motion regime, representation, or another genuine visual capability. Keep the experiments fast, allow risky or temporarily ugly results, give every technical or data-producing addition an immediate visible consumer, and avoid mistaking palette, grading, threshold, blur, brightness, or overlays for a new territory. Compare the candidates together in ordinary playback, use deterministic views only when helpful, discard weak branches, combine complementary discoveries with a clear hierarchy, modularize only what proved useful, and continue exploring from the strongest harvested state.

## Goal 3 — Representation Relay

Continuously inspect the existing result and choose whether its strongest next evolution is refinement, subtraction, reorganization, branching, or transformation among textures, fields, structured records, curves, particles, simulation, feedback, and 3D rendering. Base every choice on the actual current output and available graph data, preserve useful earlier representations, and prefer a transformation only when it opens a genuinely new visual or interactive possibility rather than adding technical novelty for its own sake. Apply analysis only when the input semantically supports it and the resulting data gains an immediate visible consumer, with depth reserved for plausible spatial evidence, optical flow for coherent motion, and Features or tracking for bounded detectable structure. Compare substantial alternatives in batches, reverse transformations that weaken the result, combine earlier outputs when they create new meaning, restore ordinary live playback after deterministic inspection, and continue from whichever representation now offers the richest next move.

## Goal 4 — Instrument-Building Loop

Begin with the existing composition's most compelling behavior, then alternate between expanding that behavior and building small performance instruments that transform it in unmistakable structural ways. Inspect the current controls, expressions, interfaces, data lanes, and motion architecture before adding anything, reuse what already works, and build a new controller or interface only when the visual behavior has demonstrated a specific expressive need. Retain only controls whose extremes create obvious compositional, topological, spatial, material, or motion-regime changes beyond ordinary Properties adjustments, and immediately use each new instrument to perform and discover the next visual development. Remove weak or redundant controls, keep telemetry contextual and truthful, test the complete system in ordinary live playback after every deterministic inspection, and continue alternating visual development with instrument development until the user says to stop.

## Goal 5 — Branching Scrapbook

Treat the existing graph as a living visual scrapbook by identifying and preserving a few strong branch roots, producing bold alternatives from them, and returning to earlier outputs whenever the current direction stops creating major visible progress. Begin by mapping the current graph's most valuable textures, structured buffers, motion behaviors, controls, and recoverable checkpoints, then keep roughly three active branches so exploration remains legible rather than sprawling. Require each branch to attempt a major structural, representational, spatial, or motion change, compare branches in batches, and crossbreed only complementary discoveries whose roles remain visibly distinct within a clear hierarchy. Delete failed branches instead of burying them beneath corrective effects, restore ordinary playback after deterministic comparisons, prune the scrapbook continuously, and finish with a graph whose retained history remains useful without becoming clutter.

## Goal 6 — Exploration Score → Retention Score

Evolve the existing composition through rapid batches of materially different experiments, using an exploration score while generating possibilities and a stricter retention score only when deciding what deserves to remain. Inspect the current live result and working graph first, preserve a recoverable baseline, then spend most of the cycle producing visible changes while limiting technical checks to quick health and meaningful-preview verification until several candidates are ready. Score exploration by novelty, structural range, motion character, expressive potential, and the quality of the next possibilities it opens, then score retention by composition, hierarchy, negative space, coherence, controllability, health, and performance. Evaluate batches rather than individual tweaks, treat vision feedback as criticism rather than permission, reject candidates that survive only as frozen frames or cosmetic corrections, restore ordinary live playback after comparison, and continue from the strongest retained state.

## Goal 7 — Hybrid Creative Evolution Loop

Read `knowledge/creative-exploration-goals.md` completely and treat its Existing-State Contract, Exploration and Evaluation Discipline, Goals 1–6, and Goal 8 as the authoritative definitions of the available creative workflows. Begin from the current Sentinel project exactly as it exists, inspect its live output and internal resources, and choose whichever documented workflow or combination of workflows is most likely to create the next major visible advance rather than executing them as mandatory stages. Briefly state the selected mode and intended visible payoff, work visibly one semantic node at a time, require immediate consumers for technical or data-producing additions, preserve and reinterpret useful earlier textures and structured buffers, and switch modes whenever the current approach stalls, degrades hierarchy, or fails to produce a major visible delta. Keep evaluation proportional to creative progress, restore ordinary live playback after deterministic inspection, continue open-ended exploration until the user says to stop, then consolidate, polish, profile, prove, and save the strongest result.

## Goal 8 — Accretive Collage Stack

Continuously evolve the existing Sentinel composition through accretive cycles in which every round creates a completely new bespoke visual element with its own internal animation, behavior, or miniature world, integrates it into the full composite, and treats that enlarged composite as the starting point for the next round. Before authoring each element, inspect the whole image and choose a distinct compositional role, scale, placement, motion language, depth relationship, and visual capability so the addition creates a major visible delta rather than another interchangeable overlay. Treat windows, panels, portals, cards, framed scenes, collage fragments, spatial layers, textured surfaces, and embedded objects as optional presentation strategies rather than a fixed aesthetic, and connect each new world to earlier outputs by reusing them as textures, masks, displacement fields, reflections, structured data, control signals, or material for further transformation. Work visibly one semantic node at a time, judge the complete composite after every insertion, vary cropping, occlusion, nesting, transparency, depth, and negative space to preserve hierarchy, consolidate or subtract when accumulation becomes clutter, and continue stacking new worlds until the user says to stop before polishing, profiling, proving, and saving the strongest layered result.
