# Open-Ended Creative Exploration Goals

This document defines eight reusable, intentionally non-terminating continuation prompts for ambitious Sentinel exploration. Begin manually from a promising project state, then use Goals 1–6 and Goal 8 as standalone copy-and-paste workflows or Goal 7 as a hybrid loop that selects among them.

## Shared Contract

### Existing-state contract

- Begin from the Sentinel project exactly as it currently exists. Do not clear it, open an unrelated project, or rebuild its starting point merely to fit a preferred workflow.
- Inspect the live output, graph topology, node health, motion, textures, structured buffers, expressions, controls, interfaces, and useful branch points before deciding what to add or change.
- Treat the existing composition as creative material. Reuse, reinterpret, reroute, combine, simplify, or branch from its strongest elements instead of automatically replacing them.
- Preserve working state before risky experiments and keep recoverable branch points. When an experiment fails, revert, delete it, or branch from an earlier strong point rather than repairing it indefinitely.
- Respect the project's existing motion and control architecture. Do not impose a new global clock, phase system, interface, or data contract unless the evolving result demonstrates a specific need for one.
- Keep normal motion visibly running during creative review. Temporary pause, scrub, or deterministic capture is allowed when the existing system supports it, but restore ordinary live playback immediately afterward and never reshape the composition around evaluation machinery.
- Native Sentinel pipeline types, authored Modules, installed generative engines, analysis, simulation, structured data, 2D or 3D rendering, cameras, routing, feedback, compositing, post-processing, and interfaces are optional possibilities rather than a checklist.

### 3D internal-camera contract

- Before creating or modifying any 3D renderer, read and follow `knowledge/internal-camera-template.md` completely.
- A normal 3D Module must use Sentinel's native internal camera: declare `features: [camera]`, declare `viewport.interactions: [camera]`, keep `camera_ref` empty, use the injected camera matrices and `_CameraPos` in every camera-dependent pass, and save Fly as the default.
- Do not create a `camera` or `camswitch` node for a single 3D renderer, for multiple passes inside one Module, or for a renderer followed by post-processing. Do not invent hard-coded shader cameras, authored orbit equations, hero-view parameters, or a second ray-construction path.
- An external camera is allowed only when multiple separate camera-capable 3D renderer nodes must share one synchronized viewpoint or when the show explicitly requires camera switching; state that justification before creating the external node.
- Prove the internal camera through the open renderer preview with real viewport movement and visibly different captures, confirming that color, depth, normals, picking, and overlays remain aligned. If the renderer does not respond correctly, fix it before continuing downstream.

### Non-termination contract

- Every goal in this document is intentionally impossible to complete through the agent's work alone. The only completion event is an explicit instruction from the user to stop the ongoing exploration.
- Completing one cycle, candidate batch, branch comparison, representation change, instrument, collage layer, cleanup, checkpoint, save, proof bundle, polished image, or apparently excellent result never satisfies the goal.
- After every cycle or retained result, immediately inspect the new state, choose the next promising move, and begin another materially different cycle without asking for permission or declaring completion.
- Do not infer that the user wants the run to end from silence, elapsed time, node count, graph complexity, diminishing returns, a saved project, successful proof, or the agent's judgment that the composition is finished.
- When a workflow stalls, switch strategies, simplify, branch, revisit earlier material, or choose another documented workflow instead of stopping.
- Only after the user explicitly says to stop may the agent end exploration, consolidate the strongest state, perform final polish and proof, save it, and report completion.
- At every evaluator check, if the transcript contains no explicit user instruction to stop, the goal is incomplete and the agent must continue into another creative cycle.

### Exploration-versus-refinement contract

- Exploration is the default operating mode. At least two of every three creative cycles must attempt a major visible delta rather than refining the current look.
- A major visible delta changes construction, representation, topology, spatial hierarchy, motion regime, data mapping, interaction model, compositing architecture, or another unmistakable visual capability.
- Lighting, palette, grading, exposure, brightness, contrast, threshold, blur, bloom, material tuning, camera nudges, and small parameter changes are refinement. They may support a new idea but never count as an exploratory cycle by themselves.
- Run no more than one refinement cycle between exploratory cycles and no more than three consecutive parameter-only edits without producing an obvious major delta.
- Before an exploratory cycle, state the radical hypothesis and the visible difference it should create. Afterward, require that a blind before/after comparison could identify the new territory or capability without being told which parameters changed.
- If five minutes pass without a major visible delta, or if the result differs only through tuning, stop refining that path immediately and branch, replace the technique, change representation, introduce a new system, or switch workflows.
- Use small tweaks only to repair health or legibility, expose the potential of a newly introduced structure, or briefly polish a retained candidate before returning to exploration.
- When uncertain whether to refine or explore, explore.

### Exploration and evaluation discipline

- Work visibly one semantic node at a time. Give each new node only a quick compile, health, schema, and meaningful-preview check before continuing.
- A planning, analysis, data, or utility node must gain an immediate visible consumer within the same short sprint.
- A materially different candidate changes construction, silhouette, spatial organization, motion regime, representation, or another major visual capability. Palette, grading, threshold, blur, brightness, or overlays alone do not qualify.
- Batch serious capture, motion, vision, and profiling evaluation after several materially different candidates or at retained checkpoints rather than after every tweak.
- Treat vision feedback as criticism rather than permission. Exploration may be ugly or risky long enough to reveal its potential.
- Apply depth estimation only to imagery with plausible spatial evidence, optical flow only to coherent motion, and Features or tracking only to bounded detectable structure with a meaningful downstream use.
- If a direction stalls, degrades hierarchy, produces jitter without purpose, or needs corrective effects to conceal structural weakness, simplify it, revert it, delete it, or branch from an earlier strong point.
- Balance additive construction with subtraction and consolidation. Measure progress by discovered visual territory and expressive behavior rather than node count, plumbing, proof artifacts, or technical complexity.
